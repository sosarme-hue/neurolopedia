Editorial approach

Rigour, sources and plain language. No alarmism, no miracle products, and no substitute for your clinician.

Sources in every article

Every article cites its sources: clinical practice guidelines (Spanish Society of Neurology, European Academy of Neurology, European Stroke Organisation, American Academy of Neurology, ILAE, NICE), peer-reviewed papers, Cochrane reviews and bodies such as NINDS, the World Health Organization, the European Medicines Agency and the Spanish Medicines Agency.

Proven, investigational and merely sold

We separate what is used in clinical practice, what is still being researched and what is sold without adequate evidence: «memory» supplements, «brain training» that promises to prevent Alzheimer's, stem-cell therapies outside clinical trials or devices that «boost» the brain.

A scan is not a diagnosis

We explain tests in context: incidental findings on an MRI, the limits of an EEG or a nerve study, and why a single result is rarely enough without the history and examination.

Corrections

Neurolopedia

An encyclopedia of neurology and neuroscience for the general public. It does not provide diagnosis or individual medical advice.

Persistent headache, memory lapses, tremor or tingling? Talk to your family doctor. If a face droops, an arm is weak or speech is slurred, call emergency services at once.
