Fourteen factors to protect your memory

The Lancet Commission estimates that almost half of all dementia cases could be prevented or delayed by acting on fourteen risk factors. What they are and what to do at each stage of life.

Text: Alejandro Rosàs, family physician

In 2024 the Lancet Commission on dementia prevention updated its report and reached a hopeful conclusion: about 45% of dementia cases worldwide could be prevented or delayed by acting on fourteen modifiable risk factors.

The fourteen factors

In early life

  1. Less education.

In midlife

  1. Hearing loss.
  2. High LDL cholesterol (new in 2024).
  3. Depression.
  4. Head injury.
  5. Physical inactivity.
  6. Diabetes.
  7. Smoking.
  8. High blood pressure.
  9. Obesity.
  10. Excessive alcohol consumption.

In later life

  1. Social isolation.
  2. Air pollution.
  3. Untreated vision loss (new in 2024).

The two that weigh most are hearing loss and high LDL cholesterol, with 7% of the risk each.

What it means

It does not mean that dementia is anyone's fault: age and genetics are still the most important factors, and many people with very healthy lives develop it. But it does mean that it is never too early or too late to look after your brain: hearing aids if your hearing is poor, glasses or cataract surgery if your sight is poor, controlling blood pressure and cholesterol, staying active, not smoking and keeping up your social life.

We explain it in detail in The 14 risk factors for dementia.
