29 October, World Stroke Day

One in four people will have a stroke in their lifetime. Recognising it and calling the emergency number within the first few minutes changes the outlook.

Text: Alejandro Rosàs, family physician

Every 29 October is World Stroke Day, promoted by the World Stroke Organization. Its message is simple: stroke can be prevented, it can be treated and, the sooner you act, the more brain is saved.

Some figures

  • According to the World Stroke Organization, one in four people over the age of 25 will have a stroke in their lifetime.
  • It is the second leading cause of death in the world and one of the leading causes of disability in adults.
  • The Spanish Society of Neurology estimates that there are between 110,000 and 120,000 strokes a year in Spain.
  • About 90% of the risk is due to factors that can be changed, with high blood pressure at the top of the list.

Recognising a stroke

Remember FAST:

  • Face: one side of the mouth droops when smiling.
  • Arms: one arm drifts down or cannot be lifted.
  • Speech: strange or slurred words, or being unable to speak or understand.
  • Time: call 112 in Europe, or your local emergency number, and note the time it started.

Sudden loss of vision, sudden unsteadiness or an explosive headache unlike any other can also be a stroke. We explain it in How to recognise a stroke.

What you can do

  • Measure your blood pressure and, if it is high, have it treated.
  • Don't smoke, move every day and keep alcohol low.
  • If you have palpitations or have been told your pulse is irregular, see a doctor: atrial fibrillation can be treated, and treating it prevents many strokes.
  • If you have symptoms that go away on their own within minutes, do not wait: it may be a transient ischaemic attack, and it is an emergency.
