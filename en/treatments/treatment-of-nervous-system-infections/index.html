Treatment of nervous system infections

Bacterial meningitis and herpes encephalitis are emergencies treated before the diagnosis is confirmed. Antibiotics, aciclovir, corticosteroids, vaccines and prevention in close contacts.

Text: Alejandro Rosàs, family physician · Updated: 26 November 2026

Treat first, confirm later

In bacterial meningitis and herpes encephalitis, time is critical. When they are suspected (fever, severe headache, stiff neck, confusion, a rash that does not fade when pressed), treatment is started within the first hour, even before the lumbar puncture if that is going to be delayed. If you suspect meningitis, call 112 (or your local emergency number).

Bacterial meningitis

The initial treatment, before the bacterium is known, depends on age:

Situation Usual empirical treatment
Adults under 50 Ceftriaxone or cefotaxime + vancomycin
Over 50s, pregnant women, people with weakened immunity The above + ampicillin (to cover Listeria)
After surgery or a head injury Vancomycin + an antipseudomonal antibiotic (cefepime, meropenem)

It is then adjusted according to the culture result. Treatment lasts 7 to 21 days depending on the organism.

  • Dexamethasone before or with the first dose of antibiotic: it reduces deafness, after-effects and mortality in pneumococcal meningitis.
  • Prophylaxis for contacts: in meningococcal meningitis, household members and close contacts receive a dose of antibiotic (ciprofloxacin, rifampicin or ceftriaxone) to prevent new cases.

Viral meningitis

This is much more common and almost always mild. It is treated with rest, painkillers and fluids. If bacterial meningitis cannot be ruled out, antibiotics are given until the results are available.

Herpes encephalitis

Encephalitis caused by herpes simplex virus is the most common severe encephalitis in Europe. It is treated with intravenous aciclovir as soon as it is suspected, without waiting for the PCR on the cerebrospinal fluid, for 14 to 21 days. Without treatment, mortality exceeds 70%; with it, it falls below 20%, although many patients are left with memory problems. It can be followed by autoimmune encephalitis, which is treated with immunotherapy.

Autoimmune encephalitis

When the immune system attacks proteins on neurons (for example, the NMDA receptor), it is treated with corticosteroids, immunoglobulins or plasma exchange, and second line with rituximab or cyclophosphamide. If it is linked to a tumour, such as an ovarian teratoma, the tumour must be removed. Recovery can take months, but is often good.

Other infections

  • Brain abscess: antibiotics for weeks and often surgical drainage.
  • Tuberculosis of the nervous system: four anti-tuberculosis medicines and corticosteroids.
  • Neurosyphilis: intravenous penicillin.
  • Shingles: antivirals (aciclovir, valaciclovir) in the first few days reduce acute pain.

Vaccines, the best prevention

Vaccines against Haemophilus influenzae type b, pneumococcus and meningococcus (C, ACWY and B) have drastically reduced bacterial meningitis in children. Vaccines against measles, polio, chickenpox and shingles, rabies, and Japanese or tick-borne encephalitis for travellers also prevent neurological disease.
