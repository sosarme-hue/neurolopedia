Non-invasive neuromodulation

Transcranial magnetic stimulation, electrical stimulation and other techniques that change brain activity from outside the skull. What they are used for and what is still research.

Text: Alejandro Rosàs, family physician · Updated: 26 November 2026

In this article

  1. What it is
  2. Transcranial magnetic stimulation
  3. Transcranial direct current stimulation
  4. Stimulation of peripheral and cranial nerves
  5. Focused ultrasound
  6. Electroconvulsive therapy
  7. Sources

What it is

Non-invasive neuromodulation covers techniques that change the activity of the brain or nerves from outside the body, without surgery, using magnetic fields, weak electrical currents or ultrasound. They harness brain plasticity: repeated over several sessions, they can produce lasting changes in circuits.

Transcranial magnetic stimulation

A coil placed on the head generates magnetic pulses that induce small currents in the cerebral cortex. In its repetitive form (rTMS) it can increase or decrease the activity of the stimulated area.

  • Treatment-resistant depression: its most established use. It is applied over the left prefrontal cortex in daily sessions for several weeks. Accelerated protocols (such as intermittent theta burst stimulation) shorten treatment to a few days.
  • Migraine with aura: a portable single-pulse device for attacks.
  • Obsessive-compulsive disorder and smoking cessation: approved in the USA.
  • Under research: rehabilitation after stroke, neuropathic pain, tinnitus, hallucinations.
  • It is also used for diagnosis: it measures the excitability of the motor pathway and maps language areas before surgery.

It is well tolerated; it can cause headache or scalp discomfort, and rarely a seizure.

Transcranial direct current stimulation

A very weak electrical current (1-2 milliamps) is applied through electrodes on the scalp. It is cheap and portable. It is being studied in depression, language and movement rehabilitation after stroke, chronic pain and cognitive decline, with promising but still variable results.

Stimulation of peripheral and cranial nerves

  • Non-invasive vagus nerve stimulation with a device on the neck: for cluster headache and migraine.
  • Supraorbital nerve stimulation (on the forehead) for migraine.
  • Remote electrical neuromodulation with a device on the arm, controlled by a mobile phone, for migraine attacks.
  • Transcutaneous electrical nerve stimulation (TENS) for some types of pain.
  • Transcutaneous spinal cord stimulation, which in 2024 improved hand function in people with cervical spinal cord injury.

See migraine treatments.

Focused ultrasound

MRI-guided high-intensity focused ultrasound can create a very precise lesion deep inside the brain without opening the skull: it is used in essential tremor and Parkinson's. At low intensity, it is being researched to modulate circuits and to temporarily open the blood-brain barrier so that medicines can reach the brain.

Electroconvulsive therapy

Although it is given under anaesthesia and triggers a controlled seizure, electroconvulsive therapy is also a form of neuromodulation, and remains the most effective treatment for severe resistant depression.
