Treatment of spasticity

The muscle stiffness that develops after a stroke, in multiple sclerosis, spinal cord injury or cerebral palsy. Physiotherapy, oral medicines, botulinum toxin and the baclofen pump.

What spasticity is

Spasticity is an increase in muscle tone that makes muscles stiff and resistant to movement, especially when they are moved quickly. It appears when the pathways running down from the brain to the spinal cord, which normally hold reflexes in check, are damaged. It is common after a stroke (affecting one in three survivors), in multiple sclerosis, spinal cord injury, traumatic brain injury and cerebral palsy.

It can cause pain, spasms, abnormal postures (bent elbow and wrist, pointed foot), difficulty walking, dressing or washing, and, in the long run, permanent shortening of muscles and tendons. It is not always treated: sometimes stiffness in the leg helps a person stand. The aim is a specific goal, such as walking better, opening the hand to wash it or sleeping without spasms.

First: physiotherapy and positioning

  • Daily, sustained and gentle stretching.
  • Strengthening the opposing muscles.
  • Splints and orthoses that keep the joint in a good position, for example an ankle-foot orthosis for foot drop.
  • Avoiding what makes it worse: pain, urinary infections, constipation, pressure sores, tight clothing or cold.

See neurorehabilitation.

Oral medicines

Medicine How it works Side effects
Baclofen Mimics GABA in the spinal cord Drowsiness, weakness; must not be stopped suddenly
Tizanidine Reduces excitation in the spinal cord Drowsiness, dry mouth, low blood pressure; monitor the liver
Dantrolene Acts directly on muscle Weakness; possible liver toxicity
Gabapentin Reduces nerve excitability Useful if there is pain or night-time spasms
Cannabinoids (nabiximols, as a spray) Endocannabinoid system Approved in multiple sclerosis when others fail

Oral medicines act throughout the body and often cause drowsiness and weakness, which limits their use when spasticity affects only a few muscles.

Botulinum toxin

When spasticity affects specific muscles (for example, the hand and elbow after a stroke), botulinum toxin injected into those muscles relaxes them for about three months without general side effects. It is repeated periodically and works best when followed by physiotherapy and splinting.

Intrathecal baclofen

In severe, widespread spasticity that does not respond to oral medicines, a pump is implanted under the skin of the abdomen that delivers baclofen directly into the fluid around the spinal cord. This achieves a strong effect with a dose hundreds of times smaller and less drowsiness. The pump is refilled every few months.

Surgery

  • Tendon lengthening and other orthopaedic operations to correct fixed deformities.
  • Selective dorsal rhizotomy: some sensory nerve roots of the spinal cord are cut; it is used mainly in children with cerebral palsy.

Questions to ask your doctor

  • What goal are we aiming for by treating the spasticity?
  • Would botulinum toxin help me?
  • Which stretches can I do at home?
