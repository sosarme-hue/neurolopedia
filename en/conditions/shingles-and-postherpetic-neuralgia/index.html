Shingles and postherpetic neuralgia

The chickenpox virus reactivates in a nerve and causes a painful rash in a band. In some people the pain lasts for months. The vaccine prevents it.

Text: Alejandro Rosàs, family physician · Updated: 26 November 2026

General information only. It does not replace advice from a health professional. For sudden neurological symptoms, call emergency services.

What it is

Shingles, or herpes zoster, is the reactivation of the chickenpox virus (varicella-zoster virus). After chickenpox, usually in childhood, the virus stays dormant for decades in the ganglia of the sensory nerves. When the body's defences weaken, because of age or illnesses or treatments, the virus reactivates and travels along the nerve to the skin.

One in three people will have shingles during their lifetime. The risk rises sharply from age 50 and in people with weakened immunity.

Symptoms

  • Pain, burning, itching or tingling in an area of skin, for 1-5 days before the rash. It can be mistaken for a heart, lung or abdominal problem.
  • A rash of red spots that turn into clusters of blisters, in a band on one side of the body only, following the area of a nerve (dermatome), most often on the chest. The blisters dry up and crust over in 7-10 days.
  • Sometimes fever, tiredness, headache.

Locations with risk

  • Ophthalmic shingles: on the forehead, around the eye and on the tip of the nose. It can damage the eye and needs urgent eye assessment.
  • Ramsay Hunt syndrome: in the ear, with facial palsy, hearing loss or vertigo.
  • In people with weakened immunity it can spread across the skin or affect internal organs.
  • Rarely, encephalitis, myelitis or stroke.

Postherpetic neuralgia

This is the most common complication: pain that lasts more than 3 months after the rash has healed. It affects 10-20% of patients, and more than 30% of people over 80. It is neuropathic pain: continuous burning, stabbing or electric shocks, and severe pain at the touch of clothing (allodynia). It can last months or years and greatly affect quality of life, sleep and mood.

Treatment

Of shingles:

  • Antivirals (valaciclovir, famciclovir, aciclovir or brivudine), ideally within the first 72 hours after the lesions appear: they speed up healing and reduce acute pain. Caution: brivudine must never be combined with fluorouracil or capecitabine (cancer drugs), because of a fatal interaction.
  • Painkillers according to the intensity of the pain.
  • Keep the lesions clean and dry. The blisters can pass chickenpox to people who have not had it or been vaccinated, especially pregnant women and babies.

Of postherpetic neuralgia:

  • 5% lidocaine or 8% capsaicin patches.
  • Gabapentin, pregabalin, amitriptyline or duloxetine.
  • Opioids only in selected cases.
  • Nerve blocks and neuromodulation techniques in pain clinics.

See treatment of neuropathic pain.

Prevention: the vaccine

The recombinant shingles vaccine (Shingrix), given in two doses, prevents more than 90% of shingles and postherpetic neuralgia, including in older people. In Spain it is included in the schedule for people aged 65 (with gradual extension to other age groups) and for adults from 18 with certain diseases or immunosuppressive treatments. Recent studies have also found a lower frequency of dementia in people vaccinated against shingles.
