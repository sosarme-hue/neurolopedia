Carpal tunnel syndrome

Tingling and numbness in the first fingers of the hand, especially at night, due to compression of the median nerve at the wrist. The most common entrapment neuropathy.

Text: Alejandro Rosàs, family physician · Updated: 26 November 2026

General information only. It does not replace advice from a health professional. For sudden neurological symptoms, call emergency services.

In this article

  2. Symptoms
  3. Risk factors
  4. Diagnosis

What it is

Carpal tunnel syndrome is the most common entrapment neuropathy. It happens when the median nerve is compressed as it passes through the carpal tunnel, a narrow channel in the wrist formed by the carpal bones and a fibrous ligament, through which the tendons that bend the fingers also pass. It affects about 3-5% of adults, more often women aged 40 to 60.

Symptoms

  • Tingling and numbness in the thumb, index, middle finger and half of the ring finger. The little finger is not affected.
  • Symptoms are typically at night: they wake the person, who shakes their hand to relieve them.
  • They get worse when driving, holding a mobile phone, reading a newspaper or with activities that bend the wrist.
  • Pain that may spread up the forearm.
  • In advanced cases: clumsiness, dropping objects, difficulty doing up buttons, and wasting of the muscles at the base of the thumb.

Risk factors

  • Repetitive wrist movements, vibrating tools, manual work.
  • Pregnancy and menopause, due to fluid retention and hormonal changes.
  • Diabetes, hypothyroidism, rheumatoid arthritis, obesity.
  • Wrist fractures, osteoarthritis.
  • Amyloidosis: carpal tunnel syndrome in both hands can be an early warning of transthyretin cardiac amyloidosis, especially in older men.
  • Long-term dialysis.

Diagnosis

  • Medical history and examination: Tinel's sign (tapping over the wrist reproduces the tingling) and Phalen's manoeuvre (bending the wrists for a minute).
  • Electromyography and nerve conduction: confirms the diagnosis, measures severity and rules out other causes (cervical radiculopathy, polyneuropathy).
  • Ultrasound of the nerve, increasingly used.

Treatment

Mild or moderate cases:

  • A night splint that keeps the wrist straight: the initial treatment.
  • Changing the activities that make it worse, taking breaks.
  • Corticosteroid injection into the carpal tunnel: relieves most people, although symptoms may return.
  • Treating causes (diabetes, hypothyroidism). In pregnancy it usually disappears after birth.
  • Oral anti-inflammatories, diuretics and vitamin B6 have not been shown to work.

Severe or persistent cases:

  • Carpal tunnel release surgery: the ligament compressing the nerve is cut, by open or endoscopic surgery, under local anaesthetic and as a day case. It resolves symptoms in more than 90% of patients. It should be done before wasting occurs, so that recovery is complete.
