Glossary

Glossary of neurology

93 neurology terms explained briefly and clearly, from acetylcholine to white matter.

ABCDEFGHILMNOPRSTVW

A

Acetylcholine
A neurotransmitter at the junction between nerve and muscle, in the parasympathetic system and in memory circuits. It is lacking in Alzheimer's disease, and its receptor is attacked in myasthenia gravis.
Action potential
A brief electrical signal that travels along the axon when the neuron fires: the nerve impulse. Read more.
Agnosia
Inability to recognise something (faces, objects, sounds) even though the sense itself works normally.
Amygdala
A small almond-shaped nucleus in the temporal lobe. It is key to fear and to giving emotional value to what we experience. Read more.
Amyloid
A protein (beta-amyloid) that builds up in plaques outside neurons in Alzheimer's disease. Read more.
Aneurysm
A bulge in the wall of an artery. If it bursts in the brain, it causes a subarachnoid haemorrhage. Read more.
Aphasia
Loss or impairment of language due to brain damage, almost always in the left hemisphere. It can affect expression, comprehension or both. Read more.
Apraxia
Inability to perform learned movements (getting dressed, using a tool) without any paralysis to explain it.
Aura
Brief, reversible neurological symptoms, mostly visual, that come before some migraines or epileptic seizures.
Autonomic nervous system
The part of the nervous system that controls, without conscious effort, the heart, blood pressure, digestion, sweating and bladder. Read more.
Axon
The long extension of a neuron that carries the nerve impulse to other cells. It can be more than a metre long. Read more.

B

Babinski sign
The big toe extends upwards when the sole of the foot is stroked. In adults it indicates damage to the central motor pathway. Read more.
Basal ganglia
Deep brain nuclei that select and regulate movements. They are affected in Parkinson's disease, dystonia and Huntington's disease. Read more.
Blood-brain barrier
A filter in the walls of the brain's capillaries that stops many substances in the blood from entering the brain. Read more.
Bradykinesia
Slowness and loss of amplitude of movement. It is the core symptom of Parkinson's disease.
Brainstem
The part that links the brain with the spinal cord: midbrain, pons and medulla. It controls breathing, heartbeat, consciousness and the cranial nerves. Read more.

C

Cerebellum
The part of the brain located behind the brainstem. It coordinates movement, balance and motor learning. Read more.
Cerebral cortex
The outer layer of grey matter of the brain, 2 to 4 mm thick, where perception, movement, language and thought are processed. Read more.
Cerebrospinal fluid
A clear fluid that bathes the brain and spinal cord, cushions them and removes waste. It is obtained by lumbar puncture. Read more.
Circle of Willis
A ring of arteries at the base of the brain that connects the carotid and vertebral circulations. Read more.
Concussion
A mild head injury with a temporary disturbance of brain function, with or without loss of consciousness. Read more.
Corpus callosum
A large bundle of fibres that joins the two cerebral hemispheres. Read more.
CT
Computed tomography, or CT scan. Fast, widely available X-ray imaging that is the first test for a stroke or head injury. Read more.

D

DaTSCAN
A nuclear medicine scan that shows the dopamine nerve endings in the striatum. It helps tell Parkinson's disease apart from essential tremor. Read more.
Deep brain stimulation
A treatment that implants electrodes in deep brain nuclei, connected to a stimulator like a pacemaker. Read more.
Dementia
A decline in memory and other mental functions severe enough to affect daily life. It is not one disease but a syndrome with many causes. Read more.
Demyelination
Loss of the myelin that coats axons, as in multiple sclerosis or some neuropathies.
Dendrite
A branching extension of a neuron that receives signals from other neurons.
Diplopia
Double vision.
Dopamine
A neurotransmitter for movement, motivation and reward. Its neurons in the substantia nigra die in Parkinson's disease. Read more.
Dysarthria
Difficulty articulating words due to poor control of the speech muscles. Unlike aphasia, language itself is intact.
Dysphagia
Difficulty swallowing.
Dystonia
Sustained muscle contractions that cause abnormal postures or twisting movements. Read more.

E

EEG
Electroencephalogram. It records the brain's electrical activity with electrodes on the scalp. Read more.
EMG
Electromyogram. It studies the electrical activity of muscles with a fine needle, and is usually done together with nerve conduction studies. Read more.
Encephalitis
Inflammation of brain tissue, due to infection or autoimmunity. Read more.
Encephalon
The whole nervous system contained within the skull: cerebrum, cerebellum and brainstem.
Epileptic seizure
A brief episode caused by an abnormal, synchronised electrical discharge of neurons. It can be focal or generalised. Read more.
Evoked potentials
A recording of the brain's electrical response to visual, auditory or sensory stimuli. Read more.

F

Fasciculation
A brief, visible twitch of a small group of muscle fibres, like a «flutter» under the skin. It is almost always harmless.
Frontal lobe
The front part of the brain: movement, expressive language, planning, judgement and control of behaviour.

G

GABA
The brain's main inhibitory neurotransmitter. Many antiseizure medicines and sedatives strengthen its effect.
Glia
The cells of the nervous system that are not neurons: astrocytes, oligodendrocytes, microglia and Schwann cells. They support, nourish and defend. Read more.
Glutamate
The brain's main excitatory neurotransmitter.
Grey matter
Tissue made up mainly of neuron cell bodies, such as the cortex and the deep nuclei.

H

Headache
Pain in the head. It can be primary (migraine, tension-type, cluster) or secondary to another cause. Read more.
Hemiparesis
Weakness on one side of the body. If it is complete, it is called hemiplegia.
Hippocampus
A structure in the temporal lobe essential for forming new memories and finding your way. It is one of the first areas damaged in Alzheimer's disease. Read more.
Hydrocephalus
A build-up of cerebrospinal fluid in the brain's ventricles, which become enlarged. Read more.
Hypothalamus
A small region at the base of the brain that regulates temperature, hunger, thirst, sleep and hormones. Read more.

I

Ischaemia
A lack of blood supply to a tissue.

L

Lacunar infarct
A small, deep cerebral infarct, less than 1.5 cm across, caused by the blockage of a fine artery.
Lewy bodies
Clumps of alpha-synuclein inside neurons. They appear in Parkinson's disease and in dementia with Lewy bodies. Read more.
Lumbar puncture
Removal of cerebrospinal fluid with a needle between two lumbar vertebrae, below where the spinal cord ends. Read more.

M

Meninges
The three membranes that wrap the brain and spinal cord: dura mater, arachnoid and pia mater. Read more.
Meningitis
Inflammation of the meninges, almost always due to infection. Bacterial meningitis is an emergency. Read more.
Microglia
The defensive cells of the nervous system, described by Pío del Río Hortega. Read more.
MRI
Magnetic resonance imaging: an imaging test that uses a powerful magnet and radio waves, without radiation, to see the brain and spinal cord in great detail. Read more.
Myelin
A fatty layer that wraps axons and greatly increases the speed of the nerve impulse. Read more.
Myoclonus
A sudden, involuntary muscle jerk, like the one sometimes felt when falling asleep.
Myopathy
A disease of the muscle itself.

N

Neurodegenerative
Describes a disease in which groups of neurons die progressively, such as Alzheimer's disease, Parkinson's disease or ALS.
Neuron
A cell that receives, processes and transmits information through electrical and chemical signals. The human brain has about 86 billion of them. Read more.
Neuropathy
A disease of the peripheral nerves. It causes tingling, pain, loss of sensation or weakness. Read more.
Neuroplasticity
The ability of the nervous system to change its structure and connections with experience or after an injury. Read more.
Neurotransmitter
A chemical that a neuron releases at the synapse to communicate with another cell. Read more.
NIHSS
A scale that measures the severity of a stroke within minutes, from 0 to 42 points. Read more.
Nystagmus
Rhythmic, involuntary movement of the eyes.

O

Occipital lobe
The back part of the brain, devoted to vision.

P

Paraesthesia
An abnormal sensation without a stimulus: tingling, pins and needles, numbness.
Parietal lobe
The upper side part of the brain: touch, body position, space and calculation.
Peripheral nervous system
The nerves and ganglia outside the brain and spinal cord. Read more.
Prodrome
Symptoms that herald an illness or an attack before it appears.

R

Radiculopathy
Damage to a nerve root as it leaves the spine, such as sciatica. Read more.
Reflex
An automatic response to a stimulus, such as the knee-jerk reflex when the knee is tapped.
Rigidity
Increased muscle tone felt when a joint is moved passively. In Parkinson's disease it can be «cogwheel» rigidity.

S

Serotonin
A neurotransmitter involved in mood, sleep, pain and migraine.
Spasticity
Muscle stiffness that increases with the speed of stretching, typical of damage to the central motor pathway.
Spinal cord
The cord of nerve tissue inside the spine that connects the brain with the rest of the body. Read more.
Status epilepticus
A seizure lasting more than 5 minutes, or repeated seizures without recovery in between. It is an emergency. Read more.
Stroke
Sudden brain damage caused by an interruption of the blood supply (ischaemic stroke or cerebral infarction, 80-85%) or by a burst vessel (haemorrhagic stroke or brain haemorrhage). «Cerebral infarction» refers only to the ischaemic type. Read more.
Stroke code
A protocol that, from the emergency call onwards, triggers urgent transfer to a hospital able to treat stroke. In Spain it is called «Código Ictus». Read more.
Substantia nigra
A nucleus in the midbrain, dark because of neuromelanin, whose dopamine neurons are lost in Parkinson's disease.
Synapse
The point of contact and communication between two neurons, or between a neuron and another cell. Read more.
Syncope
A brief loss of consciousness due to reduced blood flow to the brain, with spontaneous and full recovery. Fainting. Read more.

T

Tau
A protein that stabilises the neuron's skeleton. In Alzheimer's disease and other dementias it forms tangles inside cells.
Temporal lobe
The lower side part of the brain: hearing, understanding language, memory and emotions.
Thalamus
The brain's central relay station, which filters and distributes almost all sensory information to the cortex. Read more.
Thrombectomy
Removal of the clot from a brain artery with a catheter inserted from the groin or wrist. Read more.
Thrombolysis
A treatment that dissolves the clot in a stroke with a medicine given into a vein. Read more.
Tremor
A rhythmic, oscillating movement of a part of the body. It can occur at rest, as in Parkinson's disease, or with action, as in essential tremor. Read more.

V

Vertigo
The illusory sensation that everything is spinning or that you are spinning. It usually comes from the inner ear. Read more.

W

White matter
Tissue made up mainly of myelinated axons, which connects different areas of the brain.

Text: Alejandro Rosàs, family physician · Updated: 28 November 2026
