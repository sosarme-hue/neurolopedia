History of neurology

A timeline of neurology, from Neolithic trepanation to Cajal, magnetic resonance imaging, thrombectomy and brain-computer interfaces.

  1. c. 5000 BC

    Trepanation

    In the Neolithic, holes were drilled in the skulls of living people. Many skulls show healed bone: the patients survived.

    Read more

    Trepanation was practised in Europe, the Americas and Africa. We do not know for sure why: perhaps to treat injuries, headaches or seizures, or for ritual reasons. Dozens of trepanned Bronze Age skulls have been found in the Iberian Peninsula. It is the oldest known surgical operation.

  2. c. 1600 BC

    The Edwin Smith papyrus

    An Egyptian medical text describes the brain, the meninges and the cerebrospinal fluid for the first time, and links head wounds with paralysis.

    Read more

    A copy of an even older text, it records 48 cases of injuries, several of the skull and spine, with a surprisingly practical approach: examination, diagnosis and prognosis («an ailment not to be treated» in hopeless cases). The word «brain» appears here for the first time in written history.

  3. c. 400 BC

    Hippocrates and the «sacred disease»

    The Hippocratic treatise on epilepsy states that it is not a divine punishment but a disease of the brain.

    Read more

    «It is not, in my opinion, any more divine or more sacred than other diseases, but has a natural cause», the author writes. It also places the origin of pleasure, joy, sorrow and thought in the brain, against the idea, later defended by Aristotle, that the mind was in the heart.

  4. c. 170

    Galen and the pig that stopped squealing

    In Rome, Galen shows that cutting certain nerves in the neck silences a pig: the voice depends on nerves that come from the brain.

    Read more

    His animal experiments established that the brain controls movement and sensation. His authority lasted almost 1,400 years, errors included. Galen.

  5. 1543

    Vesalius's Fabrica

    Andreas Vesalius publishes in Basel an anatomical atlas based on human dissection, with engravings of the brain of unprecedented quality.

    Read more

    He corrected many of Galen's errors and established that direct observation outranks the authority of books. Andreas Vesalius.

  6. 1664

    Willis names neurology

    Thomas Willis publishes Cerebri anatome, illustrated by Christopher Wren, and coins the word «neurology».

    Read more

    He described the ring of arteries at the base of the brain, the circle of Willis, and placed mental functions in the substance of the brain. Thomas Willis.

  7. 1791

    Animal electricity

    Luigi Galvani reports that frogs' muscles contract when touched with metals: the nervous system runs on electricity.

    Read more

    His nephew Giovanni Aldini later gave spectacular demonstrations on corpses that are said to have inspired Mary Shelley's Frankenstein. The dispute with Alessandro Volta led Volta to invent the electric battery.

  8. 1817

    The shaking palsy

    In a short essay, James Parkinson describes the disease that now bears his name.

    Read more

    He did it with only six cases, some of them observed in the street. Charcot proposed the name Parkinson's disease half a century later. James Parkinson.

  9. c. 1810

    Phrenology

    Franz Joseph Gall proposes that each mental faculty occupies an area of the brain and can be «read» from the bumps on the skull.

    Read more

    Skull reading was a pseudoscience, but the idea that different functions have different seats in the cortex turned out to be partly right. Flourens, with animal experiments, argued the opposite: that the brain worked as a whole.

  10. 1848

    Phineas Gage

    An iron bar passes through the frontal lobe of a railway foreman, who survives with changes in his character.

    Read more

    His case, often exaggerated, became the classic example of the link between the frontal lobe and personality. Phineas Gage.

  11. 1861

    Broca and the language area

    From his patient «Tan», Paul Broca locates an area in the left frontal lobe needed for speech.

    Read more

    It was the first convincing proof that a mental function depends on a specific area of the brain. In 1874 Wernicke described the area for comprehension. Paul Broca.

  12. 1868-1882

    Charcot and the Salpêtrière

    Jean-Martin Charcot describes multiple sclerosis and ALS and, in Paris, holds the world's first chair of neurology.

    Read more

    With his anatomo-clinical method he turned neurology into a specialty. His lectures attracted doctors from all over Europe, among them Freud. Jean-Martin Charcot.

  13. 1870

    The motor cortex

    Fritsch and Hitzig stimulate a dog's cortex with electricity and produce movements on the opposite side.

    Read more

    They confirmed Hughlings Jackson's intuition that the cortex holds a map of the body. John Hughlings Jackson.

  14. 1873

    Golgi's black reaction

    Camillo Golgi invents a silver stain that reveals whole neurons.

    Read more

    Without his method, Cajal's work would not have been possible. Camillo Golgi.

  15. 1888

    Cajal and the neuron doctrine

    Santiago Ramón y Cajal shows that the nervous system is made up of independent cells that communicate by contact.

    Read more

    It is the founding idea of modern neuroscience. Golgi and Cajal shared the Nobel Prize in 1906, with opposing theories. Santiago Ramón y Cajal.

  16. 1896

    The Babinski sign

    Joseph Babinski describes the big toe reflex that reveals damage to the central motor pathway.

    Read more

    More than a century later, it is still one of the most widely used signs in medicine. Joseph Babinski.

  17. 1897

    The synapse

    Charles Sherrington names the point of contact between two neurons the «synapse».

    Read more

    His study of reflexes showed how the nervous system integrates excitation and inhibition. Nobel Prize in 1932. Charles Sherrington.

  18. 1906

    Auguste D. and Alzheimer's disease

    Alois Alzheimer presents the case of a 51-year-old woman with dementia and describes the plaques and tangles in her brain.

    Read more

    Kraepelin gave the disease his name in 1910. Alois Alzheimer · Auguste Deter.

  19. 1919

    Microglia

    Pío del Río Hortega describes microglia, the brain's defensive cells, and shortly afterwards oligodendrocytes.

    Read more

    Today microglia are central to research into Alzheimer's disease and multiple sclerosis. Pío del Río Hortega.

  20. 1921

    Chemical neurotransmission

    Using two frog hearts, Otto Loewi shows that nerves release a chemical substance to communicate.

    Read more

    The substance was acetylcholine. Nobel Prize in 1936. Otto Loewi.

  21. 1924

    The first EEG

    Hans Berger records the electrical activity of the human brain and describes the alpha rhythm.

    Read more

    He did not publish it until 1929. Since then the EEG has been the basic test for epilepsy. Hans Berger.

  22. 1927

    Cerebral angiography

    The Portuguese neurologist Egas Moniz obtains the first images of the brain's arteries using contrast.

    Read more

    It was a huge diagnostic advance. Moniz received the Nobel Prize in 1949, but for lobotomy, a psychiatric operation that caused great harm and has now been abandoned: a reminder that medicine also gets things wrong.

  23. 1937

    Penfield's homunculus

    Wilder Penfield publishes maps of the motor and sensory cortex obtained by stimulating the brains of awake patients.

    Read more

    Wilder Penfield.

  24. 1952

    The nerve impulse

    Using the giant axon of the squid, Hodgkin and Huxley explain how the action potential is generated.

    Read more

    Their mathematical model is still valid. Nobel Prize in 1963. Hodgkin and Huxley.

  25. 1953-1957

    The patient H. M.

    After epilepsy surgery, Henry Molaison loses the ability to form new memories. Brenda Milner discovers the role of the hippocampus.

    Read more

    Brenda Milner.

  26. 1967

    Levodopa

    George Cotzias shows that increasing doses of levodopa dramatically improve Parkinson's disease.

    Read more

    It was the first effective treatment for a neurodegenerative disease. Levodopa and Parkinson's medicines.

  27. 1971

    CT

    The first CT scan of a patient is performed in London, to look at a brain tumour.

    Read more

    Godfrey Hounsfield and Allan Cormack received the Nobel Prize in 1979. Magnetic resonance imaging followed shortly afterwards. CT head scan.

  28. 1986

    Nerve growth factor

    Rita Levi-Montalcini and Stanley Cohen receive the Nobel Prize for discovering growth factors.

    Read more

    Rita Levi-Montalcini.

  29. 1987

    Deep brain stimulation

    In Grenoble, Alim-Louis Benabid implants electrodes in the thalamus to treat tremor.

    Read more

    Since then more than 250,000 people with Parkinson's disease, tremor or dystonia have been treated. Deep brain stimulation.

  30. 1993

    Interferons in multiple sclerosis

    The first treatment that modifies the course of multiple sclerosis is approved.

    Read more

    Today there are more than fifteen medicines and the outlook has changed radically. Multiple sclerosis treatments.

  31. 1995

    Thrombolysis for stroke

    A trial shows that dissolving the clot within the first few hours improves recovery from stroke.

    Read more

    For the first time, stroke became a treatable emergency. Thrombolysis and thrombectomy.

  32. 2005

    Grid cells

    May-Britt and Edvard Moser discover the coordinate system of the brain's GPS.

    Read more

    Nobel Prize in 2014 with John O'Keefe. May-Britt and Edvard Moser.

  33. 2007

    Anti-NMDA receptor encephalitis

    Josep Dalmau describes a treatable autoimmune encephalitis that used to be mistaken for psychiatric disorders.

    Read more

    It opened up a whole field, that of autoimmune encephalitis. Encephalitis.

  34. 2015

    Thrombectomy

    Five trials show that removing the clot with a catheter drastically reduces disability after a severe stroke.

    Read more

    Thrombolysis and thrombectomy.

  35. 2016-2019

    Therapies for spinal muscular atrophy

    Nusinersen and gene therapy change the outlook for the leading genetic cause of death in infants.

    Read more

    Gene and RNA therapies.

  36. 2018

    Anti-CGRP medicines

    The first medicines designed specifically to prevent migraine arrive.

    Read more

    Migraine treatments.

  37. 2023

    Anti-amyloid antibodies and brain-computer interfaces

    Lecanemab is approved for early Alzheimer's disease, and a person with a spinal cord injury walks again with a digital bridge between brain and spinal cord.

    Read more

    Anti-amyloid antibodies · Brain-computer interfaces.

  38. 2025

    Blood tests for Alzheimer's and new diagnostic criteria

    The Alzheimer's Association publishes the first guideline on blood tests for Alzheimer's disease, Europe authorises lecanemab and donanemab with restrictions, and the McDonald criteria for multiple sclerosis are revised.

    Read more

    Blood biomarkers such as p-tau217 can avoid a PET scan or a lumbar puncture in people with symptoms seen in specialist clinics. The new McDonald criteria add the optic nerve, the central vein sign and kappa free light chains. A blood test for Alzheimer's · Recent guidelines.
