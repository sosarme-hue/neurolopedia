What does a neurologist do, and when will I be referred?

A neurologist treats diseases of the brain, spinal cord, nerves and muscles. How to prepare for your first appointment to get the most out of it.

Text: Alejandro Rosàs, family physician · Updated: 26 November 2026

In this article

  1. What a neurologist treats
  2. When you will be referred
  3. How to prepare for the appointment
  4. What the neurologist will do
  5. A fact
  6. Sources

What a neurologist treats

Neurology is the medical specialty that diagnoses and treats diseases of the nervous system: the brain, spinal cord, peripheral nerves and muscles. It should not be confused with psychiatry, which treats mental disorders such as depression or schizophrenia, or with neurosurgery, which operates; although the three often work together.

The most common reasons for referral are:

  • Headaches, especially migraine: the leading reason for consultation.
  • Memory problems and dementias.
  • Epilepsy and blackouts.
  • Tremor, Parkinson's and other movement disorders.
  • Stroke and its prevention.
  • Multiple sclerosis and other inflammatory diseases.
  • Tingling, neuropathic pain and weakness: neuropathies, muscle diseases, ALS.
  • Dizziness and vertigo, sleep disorders.

Many neurologists subspecialise in one of these fields, in headache, stroke, epilepsy, dementia or movement disorder units.

When you will be referred

In Spain, as in the UK, you are usually referred by your GP, who can deal with many common neurological problems. They will refer you to a neurologist if the symptoms are not typical, do not respond to treatment, need specialist tests or suggest a particular neurological disease. Emergencies, such as a stroke, are seen directly in A&E by the on-call neurologist.

How to prepare for the appointment

Appointment time is limited. Preparing helps a great deal:

  • Write down your symptoms: when they started, what they are like, how long they last, what makes them worse or better and how they have changed.
  • If you have episodes (seizures, dizzy spells, tremor), film them on your phone.
  • Keep a diary for headaches, seizures or sleep problems.
  • Bring a list of your medicines with doses, including over-the-counter medicines and supplements.
  • Bring previous reports and test results.
  • If you have memory problems or seizures, bring someone who knows you well or has witnessed them.
  • Write down your questions and start with the most important one.

What the neurologist will do

  1. A detailed medical history: the most important tool in neurology.
  2. A neurological examination: strength, reflexes, sensation, coordination, walking, cranial nerves, mental functions. Simple instruments are used, such as a reflex hammer, a torch or a tuning fork.
  3. Tests, if needed: MRI, CT, electroencephalogram, electromyography, lumbar puncture, blood tests or genetic studies.

Many neurological diagnoses (migraine, epilepsy, Parkinson's) are based mainly on the history and examination, and tests are not always needed.

A fact

According to the World Health Organization, neurological conditions are the leading cause of ill health and disability worldwide: they affect more than 3.4 billion people, over 40% of the world's population.
