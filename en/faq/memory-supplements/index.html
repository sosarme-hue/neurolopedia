FAQ

Do memory supplements work?

Ginkgo, omega-3, phosphatidylserine, vitamin complexes or nootropics: none has been shown to prevent dementia or improve memory in people without deficiencies. What does work.

Text: Alejandro Rosàs, family physician · Updated: 26 November 2026

In this article

  1. What the studies say
  2. Why caution is needed
  3. What does work to protect memory
  4. Sources

What the studies say

Pharmacies, health food shops and the internet sell many products that promise to "boost memory" or "protect the brain". However, no supplement has been shown to prevent dementia or improve memory in healthy people without nutritional deficiencies.

Supplement What the trials say
Ginkgo biloba The large GEM trial, following more than 3,000 older people for 6 years, did not reduce dementia or cognitive decline. It can increase the risk of bleeding with anticoagulants
Omega-3 (fish oil) Trials of supplements have shown no cognitive benefit in people without a deficiency. Eating oily fish as part of a healthy diet is linked to better health
Vitamin E, vitamin C No benefit in preventing dementia
B vitamin complexes Only useful if there is a deficiency (especially of B12), which should be diagnosed with a blood test
Vitamin D No clear benefit in people without a deficiency
Phosphatidylserine, choline, lecithin Little, low-quality evidence
Turmeric, resveratrol, ginseng No benefit shown in good-quality trials
"Nootropics" and stimulants Do not improve memory in healthy people and may have side effects
Apoaequorin (jellyfish protein), coenzyme Q10 and other fashionable products No good-quality evidence

A daily multivitamin showed a slight improvement in some memory tests in a US study (COSMOS), but the effect was small and its practical relevance is unclear.

Why caution is needed

  • Supplements do not go through the same checks as medicines: their actual content can vary and some contain undeclared substances.
  • They can interact with other medicines.
  • They can give a false sense of security and delay seeing a doctor about a real problem.
  • They can be expensive.

What does work to protect memory

  • Regular physical exercise: the measure with the best evidence. See physical exercise.
  • A Mediterranean diet. See diet.
  • Controlling blood pressure, diabetes and cholesterol.
  • Not smoking and limiting alcohol.
  • Sleeping well.
  • Correcting hearing loss with hearing aids.
  • Staying socially and mentally active. See cognitive reserve.
  • Getting depression treated.

Up to 45% of dementia cases could be delayed or prevented by acting on these factors. See the 14 risk factors for dementia.

Sources

  3. Baker LD, et al. Effects of cocoa extract and a multivitamin on cognitive function (COSMOS-Mind). Alzheimers Dement. 2023;19:1308-19.
