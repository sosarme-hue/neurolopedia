Brain health

The 14 modifiable risk factors for dementia

According to the 2024 Lancet Commission, almost half of dementia cases could be prevented or delayed by acting on 14 factors across the lifespan. What they are and what to do about each.

Text: Alejandro Rosàs, family physician · Updated: 26 November 2026

Almost half of dementia could be prevented or delayed

In 2024 the Lancet Commission on dementia prevention concluded that around 45% of dementia cases worldwide are linked to 14 modifiable risk factors. In other words, if they were removed entirely, almost half of dementia cases could be prevented or delayed. This does not mean every person can avoid dementia, but at population level, acting on these factors has a huge effect.

It is already happening: in several wealthy countries the proportion of older people of each age with dementia has fallen over recent decades, probably thanks to more years of education and better control of blood pressure and smoking. Even so, the total number keeps growing because we live longer.

The 14 factors

Diabetes 2%
Smoking 2%

Cholesterol and vision were added in 2024 to the twelve of 2020. The percentages do not add up exactly because the factors overlap.

Why they work

They act in several ways:

  • Protecting blood vessels: blood pressure, cholesterol, diabetes, smoking and obesity damage the small arteries of the brain and cause vascular dementia, but they also make Alzheimer's worse. See blood pressure and the brain.
  • Building cognitive reserve: education, social activity and stimulation let the brain tolerate more damage before symptoms show.
  • Avoiding direct damage: blows to the head, alcohol, pollution.
  • Keeping stimulation going: hearing and sight are the brain's input; losing them isolates it.

What you can do, by age

When young: study and keep learning, protect your hearing from noise, do not start smoking, wear a helmet.

In midlife (40-65): this is the most important time.

  • Check your blood pressure from 40 and keep the systolic below 130.
  • Keep an eye on LDL cholesterol from 40.
  • Take regular exercise.
  • Have your hearing checked and use hearing aids if you need them.
  • Stop smoking and limit alcohol.
  • Get depression treated.
  • Watch your weight and diet.

In later life: keep up social life, treat vision loss (cataracts can be operated on), stay physically and mentally active.

What cannot be changed

Age is the biggest risk factor, and so is genetics (for example, the APOE4 gene). But even people at high genetic risk benefit from a healthy lifestyle. And what is good for the heart is good for the brain: the same habits prevent stroke, heart attack and many cancers.
