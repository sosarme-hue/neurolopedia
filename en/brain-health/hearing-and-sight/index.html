Brain health

Hearing and sight

Hearing loss in midlife is the modifiable dementia risk factor with the greatest weight, and untreated vision loss counts too. Hearing aids and cataract surgery protect the brain.

Text: Alejandro Rosàs, family physician · Updated: 26 November 2026

In this article

  2. Hearing
  3. Sight

The senses feed the brain

Hearing and sight are the brain's main information inputs. When they are lost, the brain receives less stimulation, the person becomes isolated, talks less and has to work harder to understand. That is why, according to the Lancet Commission, hearing loss is the modifiable dementia risk factor with the greatest weight (around 7% of cases), and untreated vision loss was added to the list in 2024. See the 14 risk factors for dementia.

Hearing

Why deafness affects the brain

Three mechanisms are proposed, which probably add up:

  • Cognitive overload: understanding with poor hearing takes so much effort that fewer resources are left for memory and reasoning.
  • Social isolation and depression: the person avoids gatherings and conversations.
  • Atrophy from disuse: the auditory areas of the cortex get less stimulation and reorganise.

What the studies say

People with untreated hearing loss have a higher risk of dementia, while those who use hearing aids have a risk similar to people with normal hearing. In the ACHIEVE trial (2023), giving hearing aids to older people with hearing loss almost halved cognitive decline over three years in the higher-risk group.

What to do

  • Have your hearing checked from the age of 50-55, or earlier if you struggle to follow conversations in noisy places, turn the TV up a lot or ask people to repeat themselves.
  • Wear the hearing aids if they are recommended, every day and all day. Many people leave them in a drawer: it takes a few weeks to adjust.
  • If hearing aids are not enough, cochlear implants also improve cognition.
  • Protect your hearing from noise: more than a billion young people are at risk from listening to very loud music. The "60/60" rule: maximum 60% volume for no more than 60 minutes at a time. Earplugs at concerts and in noisy jobs.
  • Get earwax build-up and ear infections treated.

Tinnitus (ringing) and vertigo with hearing loss may point to Ménière's disease or other problems worth investigating.

Sight

The treatable causes

  • Cataracts: clouding of the lens, very common with age. The operation is quick and safe, and observational studies show that people who have it have a lower risk of dementia than those who do not.
  • Refractive errors: the right glasses solve most of them.
  • Glaucoma: damages the optic nerve without symptoms until late stages. It is detected by measuring eye pressure and examining the nerve.
  • Diabetic retinopathy and macular degeneration.

What to do

  • An eye examination from age 40 and every 1-2 years from 60, and every year if you have diabetes.
  • Wear the glasses prescribed and have good lighting at home, which also prevents falls.
  • Seek help immediately for sudden vision loss: it may be a stroke, a TIA or optic neuritis.

The eye, a window on the brain

The retina is nerve tissue, an extension of the brain. Examining it with techniques such as optical coherence tomography (OCT) shows thinning in multiple sclerosis, Parkinson's and Alzheimer's, and it is being researched as a simple way to detect brain diseases. See also vision.
